## 1. Purpose

This reference defines how penetration-test findings against the MAOS
substrate are **scored, classified, and routed**. It exists to guarantee
that two testers examining the same defect reach the same severity, and
that release gates respond deterministically to that severity. The
OWASP Risk Rating Methodology is the base framework; the factors below
are its standard Likelihood × Impact model **specialized to the MAOS
threat model** (Spirits, capability mediation, namespace isolation,
sandbox tiers, the transparency log, and A2A frame transport).

Severity assignment under this reference is the **single input** to the
release-blocking decision: P0 and P1 findings are release-blocking; P2
and P3 are advisory.

## 2. Severity Tiers (P0–P3)

Severity is a tiered ordinal. A finding's tier is the **maximum** of
its Likelihood band and its Impact band as scored in §4. The exemplar
defect classes under each tier are **definitional**: a finding that
matches an exemplar is classified at that tier regardless of its
computed score, and a finding that computes to a tier is classified at
that tier even if no exemplar is named. The two methods agree by
construction; both are given so that borderline cases are resolvable.

### 2.1 P0 — Critical (release-blocking)

P0 findings compromise the substrate's core security guarantees at the
kernel, trust, or persistence layer. A P0 defect means an attacker can
subvert the execution, identity, confidentiality, or integrity
foundation that every Spirit and every consumer relies on.

**Definitional exemplars** (a finding that is any of these is P0):

- **Remote code execution (RCE)** — execution of attacker-controlled
  code in the daemon, kernel-core, or any Spirit context reachable
  without prior authenticated capability, including via malformed A2A
  frames, manifest deserialization, or skill-queue payloads.
- **Authentication bypass** — acceptance of an unauthenticated or
  forged identity by the daemon admission path (`admission_view`), the
  registry, or the A2A verifier, such that an attacker speaks with the
  authority of a legitimate Spirit or operator.
- **Privilege escalation to kernel-level** — escape from a Spirit's
  sandbox or capability scope into the `maos-kernel-core` mediation
  layer, up to and including the capability mediator, security manager,
  or namespace write validator. This is sandbox escape *to* the kernel,
  not lateral movement *within* a sandbox.
- **Data exfiltration of transparency log or audit data** —
  unauthorized read, replay, or tampering of the append-only
  transparency log (`transparency_log.rs`) or audit records, breaking
  the verifiable-history guarantee the substrate owes its consumers.
- **Cryptographic key compromise** — recovery, forgery, or subversion
  of any key material handled by `maos-crypto` (`provider.rs`):
  signing keys, capability-token keys, mTLS identities, or seed material.

**Routing:** Every P0 finding generates a **blocking story** in the
sprint plan and an entry in the engagement findings log. The release is
blocked until the finding is remediated, re-tested, and closed.

### 2.2 P1 — High (release-blocking)

P1 findings break the isolation, availability, or consent guarantees
*between* Spirits or between a Spirit and its consumer, without
reaching the kernel or key material directly. A P1 defect means the
multi-tenant isolation or capability contract is violated in a way that
is exploitable and harmful, even though the substrate's root of trust
is not directly compromised.

**Definitional exemplars:**

- **Local privilege escalation within a Spirit sandbox** — a Spirit
  elevates its effective permissions *inside* its sandbox beyond what
  its manifest and issued capabilities grant, including reading or
  writing another Spirit's namespace via `validate_namespace_write`
  confusion.
- **Denial of service affecting other Spirits** — a defect that lets
  one Spirit exhaust a shared resource (CPU, memory, queue depth,
  transport) and starve or crash *co-tenant* Spirits, i.e. a
  noisy-neighbor or resource-amplification primitive that crosses the
  sandbox boundary.
- **Information disclosure of inter-Spirit communication** —
  unauthorized observation of A2A frames, queued skills, or shared
  state between Spirits, including timing or size side-channels on the
  transport that leak a co-tenant's payload metadata.
- **Bypass of capability mediation for non-critical operations** — a
  Spirit performs an operation that the capability mediator
  (`capability/mod.rs`, `security_manager.rs`) should have denied, where
  the operation is not itself P0 (i.e. not kernel escalation, not key
  compromise) but nonetheless exceeds the Spirit's granted scope.
- **Consent enforcement bypass** — an action proceeds without the
  consumer/operator consent the substrate is contracted to enforce,
  including silent capability expansion or background action outside a
  consented session.

**Routing:** Every P1 finding generates a **blocking story** and a
findings-log entry. The release is blocked until remediated, re-tested,
and closed.

### 2.3 P2 — Medium (advisory)

P2 findings represent a meaningful weakening of the security posture
that does not, on its own, violate a core guarantee. They are real
defects worth fixing, but they are not grounds to block a release.
Typical P2 findings: defense-in-depth gaps that require an additional
(unrealized) primitive to become exploitable; information disclosure
limited to non-sensitive operator metadata; missing rate-limiting on a
non-shared path; weak-but-not-broken configuration defaults.

### 2.4 P3 — Low (advisory)

P3 findings are hygiene, hardening, or documentation issues with no
direct exploitable impact. They inform future work but do not affect
the release decision. Typical P3 findings: verbose error messages that
leak no sensitive data; missing security headers on a non-sensitive
endpoint; informational note on a theoretical attack with no plausible
path under the MAOS threat model.

## 3. Release-blocking Summary

| Severity | Tier | Release decision | Story routing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Critical | **Blocks release** | Blocking story + findings-log entry |
| **P1** | High | **Blocks release** | Blocking story + findings-log entry |
| P2 | Medium | Advisory only | Advisory story / backlog |
| P3 | Low | Advisory only | Advisory story / backlog |

Only **P0 and P1** are release-blocking. P2 and P3 findings are
recorded for completeness and continuous hardening; they do not gate
the release and do not generate blocking stories. A release may ship
with open P2/P3 findings provided they are logged and owned.

## 4. Scoring Criteria

Severity is computed from two factor groups — **Likelihood** and
**Impact** — each adapted from the OWASP Risk Rating Methodology to the
MAOS threat model. Score each factor, sum within its group, and map the
group total to a band. The finding's severity tier is the **higher**
(maximum) of the two bands; the exemplar rule in §2 overrides upward
when it matches.

### 4.1 Likelihood factors

Likelihood captures how readily an attacker can reach and exercise the
defect in the MAOS substrate.

| Factor | 0 (None) | 1 (Low) | 2 (Medium) | 3 (High) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Attack surface exposure** | Not reachable | Local within a single isolated Spirit | Reachable from a co-tenant Spirit or an authenticated A2A peer | Reachable over mTLS transport from any peer, or pre-authentication at daemon admission |
| **Privileges required** | Operator-only, out of threat model | Requires an issued capability the target Spirit legitimately holds | Requires any Spirit identity or an A2A peer identity | None — unauthenticated or forged identity accepted |
| **Capability/consent interaction** | Mediator fully enforces; defect is behind it | Requires a granted capability the mediator would normally allow | Requires capability confusion or a missing mediation check | Bypasses capability mediation or consent enforcement entirely |
| **Sandbox containment** | Defect is fully contained within its sandbox tier | Escapes one sandbox control but is re-contained | Reaches another Spirit's namespace or shared resource | Reaches kernel-core mediation or crosses all sandbox tiers |
| **Ease of discovery / reproduction** | Obscure, no known reproducer | Requires skill and non-default configuration | Reproducible with a crafted manifest, frame, or queue payload | Trivially reproducible from public corpus or default topology |

**Likelihood band mapping** (sum of the five factors, range 0–15):

| Sum | Band |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Medium |
| 10–15 | High |

### 4.2 Impact factors

Impact captures the consequence to the substrate's guarantees and its
tenants if the defect is exercised.

| Factor | 0 (None) | 1 (Low) | 2 (Medium) | 3 (High) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Loss of confidentiality** | No disclosure | Non-sensitive operator metadata | Inter-Spirit communication or payload metadata | Transparency log, audit data, or cryptographic key material |
| **Loss of integrity** | No tampering | Tampering confined to the attacker's own Spirit state | Tampering with another Spirit's namespace or queued skills | Tampering with the transparency log, audit record, or capability grant |
| **Loss of availability** | None | Degraded performance for the attacker's own Spirit | Denial of service affecting co-tenant Spirits | Substrate-wide outage or daemon unavailability |
| **Escalation potential** | None, fully contained | Requires additional primitive to compound | Chains into a P1-class impact | Chains into a P0-class impact (kernel escalation, key compromise, RCE) |
| **Guarantee violated** | None | Hygiene / defense-in-depth only | Inter-Spirit isolation or capability contract | Kernel trust, authentication, transparency, or cryptographic guarantee |

**Impact band mapping** (sum of the five factors, range 0–15):

| Sum | Band |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Medium |
| 10–15 | High |

### 4.3 Severity resolution

The severity tier is resolved by combining the two bands through the
matrix below, then applying the exemplar override from §2 (an exemplar
match raises the tier to its defining tier if the matrix scored it
lower; it never lowers):

| | Impact: Low | Impact: Medium | Impact: High |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Likelihood: Low** | P3 | P2 | P2 |
| **Likelihood: Medium** | P2 | P2 | P1 |
| **Likelihood: High** | P2 | P1 | **P0** |

A finding with High Likelihood and High Impact resolves to **P0**. The
exemplar list in §2 is authoritative: for example, cryptographic key
compromise is P0 by definition even if a particular instance's factor
sum lands at the upper end of Medium.

## 5. Relationship to CVSS

Published advisories (per `SECURITY.md`) carry a **CVSS v3.1** vector
and score for external consumers. This P0–P3 tier rubric is the
*internal* pen-test severity and is the value that drives the
release-blocking decision. The two are correlated but not identical:
CVSS is computed per-instance and reported outward; the P-tier is
computed under this frozen reference and drives the engagement
workflow. Where they appear to diverge, the P-tier governs the release
gate and the CVSS score governs the advisory.
