## What this story is, in one paragraph

13.5c makes tenant mode **boot**. 13.5d makes the collective tier **reachable**. Between those two sentences sits a wall nobody costed: **`Scope::LoomRead`/`LoomWrite`/`LoomScan` exist in the kernel, are fully mediated by the kernel, are governed by the enterprise PDP — and no manifest on earth can declare them.** `capabilities_required_to_scopes` (`crates/maos-manifest/src/manifest.rs:512-540`) emits exactly two `Scope` constructors, `ProviderInfer` and `McpCall`, and it is the **sole** production feed into `manifest_scopes` (`crates/maos-kernel-core/src/security/mod.rs:310`). The policy gate is fail-closed on discriminant match (`cap_policy/mod.rs:129-143`). Therefore the first `issue_with_mediation(pid, Scope::LoomWrite, …)` any production Spirit ever makes returns `Err(CapError::PolicyDenied)`, forever, by construction.

**The framing that governs this entire story, and it is two sentences, not one:**

- **kernel-core Δ = ZERO.** The kernel mediation is *already complete* — `collective_write`/`collective_read`/`collective_scan` (`memory/mod.rs:204-270`, `:272-313`, `:315-357`) verify, audit, discriminate scope, and cap LoomWrite TTL at 60 s today. Nothing under `crates/maos-kernel-core/src/**` moves.
- **This is not a zero-delta story.** `maos-manifest` gains ≈75 LOC **and a manifest schema version bump — 3 → 4, not 3** (D1) — and that bump lands in `crates/maos-spirit-abi`, trips a hardcoded test tripwire, and drags **seven** further surfaces behind it (D2). Anyone who scopes this as *"zero-Δ, wire the adapter"* discovers it mid-implementation.

The story does **not** build 13.5c's daemon merge, 13.5e's audit isolation, 13.5b's collective erasure, or 13.3b's cross-wall recall.

## Story

As a **MAOS operator running Reza's 400-person fintech as one governed Cortex**,
I want **a Spirit to read and write the collective tier through a declared, mediated, audited route — its `loom.*` capability requirement stated in its own manifest, converted to real `Scope` values at admission, and spent as a short-lived token issued at call time through the cap-gated `collective_write`/`collective_read` that verify, journal, discriminate scope, and cap the write TTL**,
so that **the collective tier stops being a subsystem that only an xtask test can reach — and the grant that lets a Spirit touch another team's walled store is a line in a reviewable manifest rather than a mutation somebody made to the policy table at the composition root.**

## Design forks for preflight (L-numbered; grounded recommendations the ACs assume)

**L1 — the shape of `[capabilities.required.loom]`.**
- (a) Three booleans: `read = true`, `write = true`, `scan = true`.
- (b) A string list: `scopes = ["read", "write", "scan"]`.
- (c) Namespace- or team-qualified grants: `write = ["team-security/*"]`.
- **✅ Recommend (a).** `Scope::LoomRead/LoomWrite/LoomScan` are **unit variants** (`i1.rs:123,127,130`) and the policy gate matches on `std::mem::discriminant` **only** (`cap_policy/mod.rs:137-141`) — payload is *structurally ignored at this layer*. So (c) would encode a restriction **the kernel cannot enforce**: a manifest saying `write = ["team-security/*"]` would be satisfied by a write to any namespace. That is a claim standing in for a control, in the exact place this epic has killed it three times. (b) adds a string-parse failure mode for no gain. Three bools mirror the shipped `RawProviderCapabilities`/`ProviderCapabilities` Raw-then-validate pair idiom (`manifest.rs:443-471`) verbatim. **If preflight wants namespace scoping, it is a `Scope` payload change — kernel-core, FLAG-Winston, and a different story.**

**L2 — the mandatory-provider problem (D10). This fork blocks AC1 and the sketch did not see it.**
`RawCapabilitiesRequired.provider` is not `#[serde(default)]` and `validate()` rejects an empty `provider.complete` (`manifest.rs:443-450`, `:473-480`). Seven of eleven Spirits therefore omit `[capabilities.required]` entirely. A collective-only Spirit has no way to declare a Loom capability.
- (a) Make `provider` `#[serde(default)]` and relax the non-empty rule to *"at least one capability class must be non-empty"*.
- (b) Require the Spirit to declare a provider it never calls.
- (c) Put `[capabilities.required.loom]` in a **new top-level section** (`[loom]`) rather than under `capabilities.required`.
- **✅ Recommend (a), with a proven-red that a wholly empty `[capabilities.required]` is still rejected.** (b) manufactures a false `Scope::ProviderInfer` grant to satisfy a parser — a real capability widening as a side effect of a schema shape, which is precisely the failure this epic exists to prevent. (c) is cleaner TOML but forfeits the `capabilities_required_to_scopes` chokepoint: the whole value of AC1 is that **one** function is the sole feed into `manifest_scopes` (`security/mod.rs:310`), and a second section means a second feed. **Note (a) is a relaxation of a shipped validation** — it needs its own negative and its own line in the change log, and it is the reason AC1 is bigger than "add a field."
