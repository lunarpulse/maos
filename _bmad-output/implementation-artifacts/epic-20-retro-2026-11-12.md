### 10. Key insights and significant discoveries

1. **The kernel's T3 admission path is not on the daemon's production path** (C13 boundary 1) — a security-relevant discovery about *where* a control runs, found only by trying to prove it end to end. Until an owner exists, NFR-Sec-15 evidence for "crypto provider selected → T3 admission" rests on a bin-side recording test, not on a daemon run.
2. **A control can be real in the kernel and unreachable from the artifact**: the same pattern as 20-1b (CRL → Worker class-less SCB). Both are fixed by deciding *what the product binary does*, not by more kernel tests. This is exactly what the lane's own success criterion ("FRs LIVE from a product binary", `sprint-change-proposal-2026-09-04.md:104`) measures; see §12 and Amendment A1.
3. **Hosted proofs are expensive and cannot be batched**: each of 20-9's seven recipe runs (43179221668 … 43217945522) found one more recipe or product problem (C9 lines 14–15). Budget one hosted cycle per hosted AC at creation (E19-A3 carried forward).
4. **The pre-tag checklist, not the tag, is the long pole.** Ruling 13 condition (b) needs a green main `aggregate` at the candidate SHA while two recurring flakes red it (§7); condition (c) needs a ~26 h chain that has never run; (e) needs a candidate-SHA `release.yml` rehearsal.
5. **A "done" flip is a tree change**: it moves the d11/recovery-lane pins (`76ae19c9`) and exposes record-state findings (20-4 revert `345ae30f`). Flip in the same commit that carries the evidence, then re-run `d11_xtask_ceiling_ratchet` + `recovery_lane_ceiling_rule` (ACC item 1, `proof-contract.md:40-44`).
6. **Stale-owner hazard:** dropping a key as work while 95+ rows name it as Owner keeps `check-dev-record-completeness` green but hides recurring reds in a lane nobody will do (§7, D-E20-2).
7. **Trigger-owned rows need a discharge step**: the `archive.rs` doc row's trigger fired at 20-9 L2 and nothing discharged it (§2, E19-A6).
