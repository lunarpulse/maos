## What Was Challenging

### 1. Story 9.5 was scoped too broadly (split into 5 sub-stories)

The original 9.5 had 8 ACs covering docs, WCAG, i18n, tooling, onboarding, OTel, AND trust-anchor ADR. That's at least three distinct jobs-to-be-done. It was the "everything else" bucket in the original 5-story epic. The preflight correctly decomposed it, but the initial story creation should have caught this. The commit message (`866ed50 unfinished -...`) and 6 deferrals that became 2 new stories (9.5c, 9.5d) are the cost evidence.

### 2. Non-Opus models: safety net works, but review cost is 2-3x

Review patch count by dev model:

| Model | Story | Patches | CRITICALs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kimi-for-coding` | 9.2 | **30** | **4** | Legal-hold non-persistent, theatrical proofs |
| `openai-codex/gpt-5.4` | 9.5d | **25** | 0 | Tautological gates, one-directional checks |
| `zai/glm-5.2` | 9.6 | **21+** | 0 | Compile-time tautology oracles, Mira unwired |
| `claude-opus-4-6` | avg of 7 | **~15** | 0-1 | Consistent, predictable |
| `claude-opus-4-8` | 9.4b | **9** | **0** | Most complex story, fewest patches |

The §A6 safety net caught everything — no production gaps escaped review. But **200+ patches across 14 stories** means review is consuming a significant fraction of total effort. Non-Opus stories account for a disproportionate share. The pattern is consistent with the `deepseek-v4-pro` memo: non-Opus models are strong on domain logic, weak on absence-of-path reasoning, integration plumbing, and systemic invariants.

### 3. Sprint-status tracking slipped on 2 stories

Stories 9.5 and 9.7 were `done` in their story files but `in-progress` in sprint-status.yaml. Caught at retro, corrected. The dashboard was wrong for 2 days.

### 4. 14 stories exceeded the 3-5 per-epic sizing target

Original 5 + retro-added 9.6 = 6 base stories. Each split produced the 14 total. The splits were healthy decomposition (not scope creep), but 14 stories stresses tracking overhead. The split discipline is a process improvement, but the initial story creation needs to anticipate it.

### 5. Kernel baseline required 4+ re-pins

Progression: 21128 → 21197 → 21336 → 21472 → 21894 → 22226 → 22227. Each authorized and FLAG-Winston'd. The single-source-of-truth from §A4 is working — we can see the growth honestly — but +1099 lines (~5.2%) across 4 deltas is worth monitoring.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Compounding gates are the most important process innovation.** Three epics validated: gates that ship complete in one story hold; promises that depend on backfill decay. Zero exceptions. Apply this forward to every Epic 10 commitment.

2. **Proven-red is the most load-bearing review rule.** Every story in Epic 9 required proven-red fixes in review. Promoting it from review checkpoint to dev-pass gate (§A1) is the highest-leverage review-cost reduction.

3. **Model selection is a review-cost decision.** `claude-opus-4-8` on the most complex story produced 9 patches. Non-Opus on simpler stories produced 21-30. For Epic 10's certification gates, this is not a luxury — it's risk management.

4. **Split discipline is mature but initial sizing needs work.** Six clean splits are a process win. But 14 stories from 5 originals means the initial scoping wasn't catching decomposition needs. The 6-AC guard (§A5) targets this.

5. **The 3-epic carry-forward is the anti-pattern; the hard gate is the cure.** Epic-7 §A3 (skill-queue) survived three epics of "verify before assuming." It died in one story when the retro was gated on its completion. Apply this to any item at risk of decay.
