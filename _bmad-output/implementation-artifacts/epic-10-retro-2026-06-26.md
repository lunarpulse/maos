## 2. What Went Well

1. **Derive-and-reconcile killed the self-reporting trap.** 10.2's trial gate originally trusted `successes=12` self-reported — `participant=[] + successes=12` would have *passed* a blocking v1.0 gate. Same class in 10.4a's migration gate and 10.4b's consent floor. All three caught by adversarial review; counts are now derived per-record everywhere. **A gate that reads a number from a TOML file is a press release, not a gate.**
2. **"Flip the feature ON" ≠ measurement.** 10.4b proved at HEAD that `j4.rs::run_j4_kernel` returned canned samples (`1000 + (i*20)%5000`, always under 10ms) *even with `kernel_measurement` ON* — the premise carried from 10.2 was false (17 ABI-drift errors in the real harness). 10.4c rebuilt the real `scalar.tap` harness from the gold-standard template; P95 is now **1µs measured** and **falsifiable** (mutation injects ≥15ms → gate goes red).
3. **Guard by type, not by config.** 10.4b's confused-deputy proof *had* to drive the live `handle_intake_verified` TCP path — `LoopbackA2ARouter` physically cannot exhibit the bug (host_id binding enforced only on real TLS). Forged `from.host_id ≠ verified peer` → REJECT, RED on loopback by construction.
4. **"WOULD HAVE BLOCKED at v1.5" banner = auditable hold.** No waiver registry, no tag machinery — the banner *is* the hold. Operator sees it every run and makes a conscious call. Disposition lives in one place: `{v1_0 = "advisory", v1_5 = "blocking"}` in `gate-registry.toml`, consumed by the existing aggregate.
5. **Tripwire + FLAG-Winston enforced intentional kernel deltas.** Both 10.4a and 10.5 re-pinned the baseline explicitly; the "stop if kernel src moves by one line" tripwire caught zero accidental overflow.
6. **Epic 9 retro follow-through was the best to date.** §A3 (daemon admission enforcement + `validate_namespace_write` de-stub) closed in commit `9b8d208` (2026-06-19) **before** the pen-test work began — the compounding-gate law working: ship the hard gate before its dependency needs it, and it doesn't decay.

## 3. What Was Hard / Systemic Issues

1. **First-pass review findings often not applied (4 stories).** 10.2's re-review found every prior patch checkbox unchecked; 10.4a's de-stub was inert and I1/I2 mediation unwired until the second adversarial pass reworked it. The second pass is **structurally necessary** — budget for it, don't hope to skip it.
2. **Model-tier policy drift.** §A2 (Epic 9 retro) mandated **opus-4-8 for Tier-1** stories (10.1, 10.2, 10.4). In practice 10.1b, 10.2, 10.4a, 10.4b, and 10.4c all ran on **opus-4-6**. Outcomes were acceptable *only because* the §A6 multi-layer review net caught what the cheaper model missed (inert de-stubs, unwired mediation, loopback-can't-fail proofs, canned numbers). We were relying on adversarial review to backstop a model policy we weren't following.
3. **Degraded review on the riskiest story.** 10.5 carried **7 ACs + 355 LOC of unsafe Windows FFI** in the kernel, but its review subagents hit Anthropic rate-limits and failed — main-thread review only. 10.4c also dropped to 3-layer (no Test-Infra). The two stories with the weakest review coverage are among the highest-risk in the epic.
4. **Operational holds masquerading as "done."** v1.0 ship requires a real external pen-test engagement returning zero P0/P1 — 10.1b built the *harness* and a conditional (advisory-if-absent) gate, but the records do not show a real engagement result. Export-compliance counsel confirmation is a pre-distribution gate, still open. Korean SR manual runbook was due *before* 10.3 shipped (10.3 is done → overdue).
5. **No next epic to prep against.** Epic 11/12 were never elaborated in the planning artifacts — post-v1.5 lives only as PRD *phases* (v2.0 technical, v2.5 ecosystem). The "prep for next epic" step is actually a "decide what the next epic is" step.

## 9. Key Takeaways

1. **Derive-and-reconcile, real-subsystem proven-red, and "feature-flag ≠ measurement"** are now the project's gate-building reflexes — they caught fabricatable gates in three separate stories.
2. **The §A6 review net is load-bearing, not optional** — it backstopped a model-tier policy we didn't follow. Going forward, risk-gate the model per story (§A5) and never let review degrade silently on high-risk code (§A6).
3. **"Done at story level" ≠ "shippable."** Calling v1.5 sealed before the pen-test engagement, 10.5 re-review, and export counsel close would be the exact self-reporting trap we spent the epic eliminating. The release-hold ledger keeps it honest.
4. **The roadmap ran out of defined epics.** Formalizing v2.0 as Epic 11 before further dev prevents discovering the Cortex/Loom foundation gaps mid-story.
